# Usefulness

In our previous articles, we explored the concepts of **User-centered Design (UCD)** and **Design Thinking**. While UCD provided a theoretical foundation and mindset for creating user-centric products, Design Thinking offered a practical implementation of UCD principles through a structured process. Building upon these ideas, we now delve into the concept of **usefulness** - a concrete measure of how well an application meets user requirements in terms of both **utility** and **usability**. In our company, we excel at fulfilling functional requirements, ensuring that our products offer the features and capabilities users need. However, we often struggle with meeting usability requirements, which focus on how easily and efficiently users can interact with those features. This gap can significantly impact the overall usefulness of our applications. 

In this article, we will explore the dual aspects of usefulness - utility and usability - and discuss ways in which we can strive to enhance usability within our company. By improving usability, we can elevate the overall usefulness of our products, creating solutions that not only meet functional requirements but also provide a seamless and delightful user experience.
